It started as my graduation project at Arab Open University in Muscat: an agentic AI assistant
for our own university. Students ask the same questions every semester about enrolment, courses
and policies, and the answers usually sit in a queue until someone in the office is free. We
wanted something that answers right away, any time of day, so staff can spend their time on the
cases that really need a person.

Then the project became research. The project was funded by Oman's Ministry of Higher Education,
Research, and Innovation, and we wrote up the system as a paper, "Next-Generation Student
Services: Developing an AI Assistant for Enhanced University Support". It was accepted at ICTIM
2025 in Malaysia, part of the Digital Futures International Congress, and I presented it there.
I was lucky to win the Best Presenter award.

The work kept going after that. Our second paper looked at a harder problem: making the assistant
reliable in the way our students actually talk, in English, Modern Standard Arabic, Franco Arabic
and Omani Arabic. We fine-tuned it in two stages, first for facts and then for safely refusing
questions it should not answer. That paper was presented at ACET 2025 and is now on IEEE Xplore.

And this month the first paper came out as Chapter 15 of "Intelligent Governance in the Big Data
Era", in Springer's Studies in Big Data series.

A project that began as a requirement to graduate is now a chapter in a book. Thank you to my
co-authors Dr. Mohammad Abrar and Samaa Al Hasani, and to everyone at AOU who supported it.
